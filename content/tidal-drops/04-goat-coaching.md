# Tidal Drop 04 — GOAT Coaching (Go Out And Try)

**Seed:** Insight doesn't come from more analysis indoors — it comes from going out and trying. The goats at Casa Lea learn the cliff by climbing it.

---

## 1. Short-form video script (faceless)

[visual: dawn fog on the cape, a goat stepping onto a granite ledge]

There's a goat on this cliff who has never once studied the cliff.

She doesn't survey it. She doesn't map the footholds or wait for better weather.

[visual: close on hooves finding a hold in the rock]

She puts one hoof on the granite. The granite answers. She adjusts.

That's the whole method. Contact. Feedback. Next step.

[visual: wide shot — the cape, the Atlantic moving below]

I used to believe insight was something you produced indoors. More frameworks. More planning. One more pass at the plan before risking the first move.

But the goats keep teaching the same quiet lesson: you cannot think your way across terrain you've never touched. The cliff is only knowable from the cliff.

[visual: boots on a coastal path, walking away from a desk left behind]

Movement isn't what happens after the thinking. Movement *is* the thinking. The body runs experiments the mind can't simulate.

[visual: the goat at the top, unbothered, chewing]

So when a question won't resolve at the desk, it isn't asking for more analysis.

It's asking for ground.

Go out. And try.

## 2. Written post (X / LinkedIn)

The goats here have never read a book about climbing.

Every morning they take the same cliff that makes visitors go quiet. No plan. No survey of the footholds. One hoof on the granite — the granite answers — adjust.

Contact. Feedback. Next step. That's their entire method, and it has never once failed them.

I spent years doing the opposite. When something felt uncertain, I added analysis. Another framework, another document, one more pass at the plan. It felt like rigor. It was mostly a sophisticated way of not touching the rock.

Systems thinking has one quiet rule: a complex system reveals itself only when you interact with it. You can't map the cliff from the kitchen window. The terrain holds information that exists nowhere else — not in your notes, not in your models — and it only speaks to a body in motion.

The goats aren't brave. They're in conversation with the ground.

Around here we call it GOAT coaching: Go Out And Try.

The cliff is only knowable from the cliff.
